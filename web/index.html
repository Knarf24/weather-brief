🌦️ Daily Weather & Air Brief

Weather apps show a wall of numbers. This gives you the few things you need before you leave home: do you need an umbrella and when will it rain, what to wear, how clean the air is and the best time to walk, run or let the kids play outside. Get it on the page, or every morning on Telegram or WhatsApp.

🐍 The brief is written by Python running right here in your browser with Pyodide, using free forecasts from Open-Meteo.

      📲 Get it every morning

      The same Python code runs on GitHub every morning and sends a short 3-line brief to your phone in the morning, afternoon and evening (or the hours you choose, in your city's time). It's free. Make your own copy of the project on GitHub, then pick one:

      📱 Phone notification (easiest)

      1. Install the free ntfy app (Android or iPhone).
      2. Tap + and subscribe to a topic name that's hard to guess, like wb-7f3k9q2m8x.
      3. On GitHub add the secret NTFY_TOPIC with that same name.

      Telegram

      1. In Telegram, message @BotFather, send /newbot and copy the token it gives you.
      2. Send any message to your new bot, then open https://api.telegram.org/bot<token>/getUpdates and copy the number after "chat":{"id":.
      3. On GitHub add the secrets TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN and TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID.

      WhatsApp (via CallMeBot)

      1. Open CallMeBot's WhatsApp page, save the bot's phone number shown there in your contacts, and send it I allow callmebot to send me messages on WhatsApp. (If the page says the bot is full, try again in a few days, or use Telegram.)
      2. It replies with your API key.
      3. On GitHub add the secrets CALLMEBOT_PHONE (your number, like +27821234567) and CALLMEBOT_APIKEY.

      CallMeBot is a free personal service, fine for messages to yourself. The official WhatsApp Business API costs money.

      Set your city and hour as the repository variables CITY and SEND_HOURS (like 6,12,17) (Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions → Variables). Secrets stay private on GitHub; this page never sees them.

      How it works

      1. Find the place: Python looks up your city with Open-Meteo's free geocoder.
      2. Weather: it fetches today's hourly forecast (rain chance, temperature, wind, UV) and decides whether you need an umbrella and when the rain is most likely (GeeksforGeeks Scraping Weather Data for an Umbrella Reminder).
      3. Air quality: it fetches forecast pollutant levels and works out the Air Quality Index itself with the EPA breakpoint formula, naming the main pollutant and what to do (GeeksforGeeks Predicting Air Quality Index).
      4. Best time outside: it scores every hour by rain chance, air quality and heat and picks the best stretch.
      5. Morning message: a GitHub Actions job runs the same Python every morning and sends the brief to Telegram or WhatsApp (GeeksforGeeks Automate WhatsApp Messages).

      🔒 Privacy: no account and nothing saved except your recent cities, in this browser only. Your city (or location, if you tap 📍) is sent to Open-Meteo to get the forecast.

      Weather and air data: Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0), air quality from CAMS (Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service). Forecasts can be wrong; for severe weather follow your national weather service.
